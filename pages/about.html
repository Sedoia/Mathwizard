About the project

A 3D mobile game for learning calculus

Math Wizard helps engineering students at the National College of Science and Technology (NCST) Dasmariñas practice integral and differential calculus. Students solve derivative and integral problems to cast spells and defeat enemies, so practice happens inside the game.

Capstone project, BSIT Computer Studies Department Version 1.0.4

The problem

Many NCST engineering students find calculus too theoretical. Calculus is the base of later engineering courses, but students often lack ways to visualize the concepts and practice them outside class. That leads to low motivation and weak exam results.

Our objectives

  • Design and develop a 3D mobile game focused on derivatives and integrals.
  • Identify the difficulties NCST engineering students face with abstract calculus concepts under traditional teaching.
  • Determine whether the game helps students understand calculus fundamentals.

How it works

Math Wizard has two parts. This website handles account registration and distributes the Android installer. The 3D game runs on the student's phone. Players choose between two modes:

  • Story mode is single-player. It works offline, and the Local Authority checks answers on the device.
  • PVP mode lets players battle each other online. It needs an internet connection, and the Network Authority confirms each move.

In both modes, enemy encounters present calculus problems. A correct answer restores or raises health and energy. A wrong answer reduces them.

Login module

Account sign-in, registration and password recovery.

Main menu and UI

Story mode, PVP mode, profile and settings. A centralized UI module manages screen changes.

Player control

Movement, spell casting, and health and energy tracking.

Enemy module

Enemy spawning and behavior. Enemies are reused (pooled) to keep performance steady.

Math module

The Math Generator produces pre-programmed calculus problems, and Math Authority checks each answer.

Authority module

The game's referee. Local Authority covers offline play and Network Authority covers online matches.

Requirements and limitations

  • Android 12 or higher with at least 4 GB of RAM. iPhones are not supported.
  • PVP mode needs an internet connection. Without one, players can use Story mode only.
  • The game covers integral and differential calculus only.
  • Problems are pre-programmed and do not adapt to other lessons.
  • Testing is limited to selected NCST engineering students.

Built with

  • Unity and C# for the 3D game, written in Visual Studio Code.
  • Blender for characters, enemies and level assets.
  • Google Firebase for authentication, profiles and data storage.
  • PayMongo (GCash and PayMaya) for in-game credit top-ups on this website.

Evaluation

100 beta testers evaluate the game: 90 NCST engineering students, 5 engineering faculty and 5 IT experts. They answer a Google Forms questionnaire on a 5-point Likert scale covering performance, interface design, accuracy, security and instructional value.
